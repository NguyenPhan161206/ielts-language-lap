## Part 3 — Read and choose True / False / Not Given

### Creating an Ergonomic Workplace

**A.** Many companies are now rethinking how their office spaces are organized. The aim is to help employees work more comfortably and stay free from injury. Simple improvements like better chairs and adjustable desks can make a big difference. These changes are based on studies that look at how the human body moves and rests while working. When offices are set up to suit the body's natural positions, workers are less likely to experience back or neck pain. Over time, this can lead to fewer sick days and higher job satisfaction. In addition to physical comfort, ergonomically-designed spaces can also support mental focus by removing distractions or discomfort. Companies that prioritize ergonomic solutions tend to notice better retention rates and employee morale. These efforts are part of a larger trend toward creating a healthier lifestyle at work.

**B.** A key aspect of setting up a good office is making sure people can keep a healthy sitting position throughout the day. This includes having screens at the right height and chairs that support the lower back. If workers sit in an awkward position, it can cause discomfort or even long-term pain. That's why many businesses invest in tools that can be customized to the user's needs. Experts are sometimes brought in to study how the work is done and suggest better layouts. This process is known as a professional review of work habits. These assessments help determine whether changes in layout or equipment are needed. Companies that perform these evaluations often find areas to improve efficiency and reduce physical strain. Ergonomics assessments have become a standard practice in some industries.

**C.** Another goal of designing a better workspace is to help people feel more energetic and focused. A comfortable environment can help reduce tiredness and muscle tension. For example, bright lighting and fresh air can make workers feel more alert. Some offices now include green plants or quiet zones to support mental clarity. These changes don't just make the workplace look nicer; they help people feel better and work better. Adjusting settings such as desk height or monitor angles can also increase comfort and support better concentration. These small modifications can contribute to a healthier lifestyle during the workday. Workers who feel comfortable are more likely to stay motivated and complete tasks effectively.

**D.** Aside from furniture and lighting, it's also important to think about how often people take breaks. Short walks or simple stretches during the day can improve blood flow and help avoid stiffness. Encouraging these habits is part of promoting a more active and health-conscious work life. When companies support their employees in staying physically and mentally fit, it reflects in their overall attitude and productivity. Some organizations even design systems that include reminders for posture checks or scheduled movement. These methods aim to reduce the risk of muscle strain and improve the work environment. Promoting movement throughout the day can prevent fatigue and support long-term well-being.

**E.** As interest in worker health grows, more attention is being paid to making smarter choices at work. That includes offering better food options in the cafeteria and ensuring workspaces meet certain safety and comfort standards. Some companies even redesign their office systems to match how people naturally move and work best. These design strategies aim to make the office a place that supports both task performance and personal well-being. A healthier work environment leads to improved efficiency and fewer physical complaints among staff. Businesses also consider using office equipment that adjusts automatically based on user habits. By applying sound design principles, workplaces can better serve the needs of their employees and encourage healthier choices.

### Questions

| # | Statement | Answer | Evidence (paragraph) |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| 1 | Some companies design their offices to help workers reduce injuries. | **TRUE** | A: "stay free from injury"; "Some companies..." = "Many companies are now rethinking..." |
| 2 | Adjustable office equipment is more expensive than traditional furniture. | **NOT GIVEN** | Không đoạn nào đề cập giá/chi phí |
| 3 | Providing ergonomic tools can lead to greater employee satisfaction. | **TRUE** | A: "higher job satisfaction"; "better retention rates and employee morale" |
| 4 | Evaluating workplace ergonomics is not a routine procedure in some fields. | **FALSE** | B: "Ergonomics assessments have become a standard practice in some industries" — mâu thuẫn trực tiếp |
| 5 | Adjusting desk height has a positive effect on the employee's mental health. | **TRUE** | C: "Adjusting settings such as desk height... increase comfort and support better concentration... healthier lifestyle" |
| 6 | Some companies provide tools that remind employees to move and check their posture. | **TRUE** | D: "systems that include reminders for posture checks or scheduled movement" |
| 7 | All companies now use equipment that adapts to individual users. | **FALSE** | E: "**Some** companies / Businesses **also consider**..." — quantifier "All" bị strengthening |

**🔑 Answer key:** 1-T, 2-NG, 3-T, 4-F, 5-T, 6-T, 7-F

**📝 TFNG strategy reminders:**
- *False* = mâu thuẫn trực tiếp với text (từ khóa đối lập: *not routine* vs *standard practice*; *All* vs *Some/consider*).
- *Not Given* = text hoàn toàn không đề cập (kiểm tra keyword: giá tiền không xuất hiện → NG).
- Cảnh giác với **quantifiers** (all/some/always/often) — nguyên nhân phổ biến nhất của False.

## Part 4 — Summarize two stories using learned words

**Instructions:** Viết đoạn tóm tắt 2 câu chuyện, sử dụng từ vựng đã học. (Bài tự luận — chấm điểm bằng AI.)

### Story 1: Graphic Designer

> Julia works as a graphic designer and spends most of her day at a desk. A few months ago, she started having pain in her shoulders and wrists. Her company decided to bring in a specialist to check how their workstations were set up. As a result, the team made some simple changes—like moving the screens to eye level and offering chairs that better support the lower back. Julia also learned how to sit properly and take regular breaks. These small improvements helped reduce her discomfort and allowed her to focus more easily throughout the day.

➡ **Target words:** ergonomics, posture, efficiency

**范文 (Sample summary):**
> After an **ergonomics** assessment of her workstation, Julia corrected her sitting **posture**—screens at eye level and a chair supporting her lower back. With regular breaks, her discomfort decreased and her work **efficiency** improved noticeably.

### Story 2: Well-being in fitness

> Michael is a gym coach who recently changed the way he plans workouts for his clients. Instead of only focusing on muscle building, he now includes stretching and breathing exercises. He noticed that his clients are more focused during the day. To better support their physical and mental health, he designed a weekly plan that allows adjustments based on each client's ability. By creating routines that match the body's natural rhythm, Michael helps people stay active without pushing them too hard. His goal is to encourage long-term fitness and overall well-being.

➡ **Target words:** healthier, well-being, strain

**范文 (Sample summary):**
> Michael redesigned his training plans so clients can **adjust** each session to their ability, preventing muscle **strain** while building fitness. The result is **healthier**, more focused clients whose overall **well-being** has improved over time.
