## How to use this bank

1. **Warm-up (3 min):** classify each prompt by tone (informal / semi-formal / formal) + purpose → answer table at the bottom.
2. **Draft (10 min):** write only the *skeleton* (opening → 3 bullets → closing) first.
3. **Write (20 min):** full letter ≥ 150 words. Use only safe relationship words (`to / because / before / if / although / so`).
4. **Grade:** AI-graded, or self-check with the 4 criteria.

## A. Informal Letters (friends, family — contractions OK, warm tone)

**A1. Weekend Visit**
You are going to visit your best friend in another city for a long weekend. Write a letter to your friend:
- Tell them when you will arrive and how long you will stay.
- Ask if they can suggest some places to visit.
- Arrange where and when to meet.

**A2. New Apartment**
You have just moved into a new apartment. Write a letter to a friend:
- Describe the new apartment.
- Give a reason why you chose this place.
- Invite your friend to visit soon.

**A3. House-sitting**
You are going away for two weeks. Write a letter to your cousin asking them to look after your house:
- Explain why you will be away.
- Describe what you need them to do (plants, pets, post).
- Offer to return the favor.

**A4. Thank You Gift**
A friend gave you a valuable gift for your birthday. Write a letter to thank them:
- Thank them and describe how you used the gift.
- Explain why the gift is special to you.
- Invite them for a celebration dinner.

**A5. Borrowed Book (informal version)**
You borrowed a bicycle from a friend and crashed it. Write a letter:
- Apologize for the damage.
- Explain what happened.
- Say what you will do to make it right.

**A6. Surprise Trip**
You are planning a surprise trip for your family. Write a letter to your sibling:
- Explain the plan and destination.
- Ask them to help you keep it secret.
- Suggest who else could join.

**A7. Airport Pick-Up**
You are returning to your hometown after studying abroad. Write to your uncle:
- Tell him your arrival date and time.
- Ask if he can pick you up at the airport.
- Suggest a plan for your first day back.

**A8. Gym Partner**
You are starting a gym program. Write to a friend:
- Describe the program you have chosen.
- Ask your friend to join you.
- Propose regular training times.

## B. Semi-Formal Letters (known people of respect: teacher, neighbor, landlord, supervisor you know)

**B1. Lost Textbook**
You borrowed a textbook from your teacher and lost it. Write a letter to your teacher:
- Apologize for losing the book.
- Explain how it happened.
- State what you will do about it (replace, find it).

**B2. Noisy Neighbor**
Your neighbor regularly plays loud music at night. Write a letter to your neighbor:
- Explain the problem.
- Say how it affects you.
- Suggest a solution (e.g., quiet hours).

**B3. Broken Heater (landlord)**
The heater in your rented apartment is broken in winter. Write a letter to your landlord:
- Describe the problem.
- Explain how it affects your daily life.
- Ask them to repair it quickly.

**B4. Day Off Request (your supervisor)**
You need a day off next week for a personal reason. Write a letter to your supervisor:
- Explain the reason for the leave.
- Say what you will do before you leave.
- Propose who can cover your tasks.

**B5. Shift Swap (colleague)**
You cannot work your shift on Friday because you have an appointment. Write a letter to your work colleague:
- Explain why you need to change the shift.
- Ask them to swap with you.
- Offer to return the favor next week.

**B6. Coach Feedback**
Your football coach changed the training schedule. Write a letter to your coach:
- Thank them for the new plan.
- Give your opinion about the training.
- Suggest one small improvement.

**B7. Apology to Professor**
You missed an important class because of a family emergency. Write a letter to your professor:
- Apologize for missing the class.
- Explain the reason.
- Ask how you can catch up on the material.

**B8. Roommate Request**
Your roommate is leaving the city for a month. Write a letter to your roommate:
- Ask them how to manage the rent while they are away.
- Suggest a plan you both can agree on.
- Mention any house rules you want to keep.

## C. Formal Letters (unknown recipients / organizations: manager, store, bank, airline, council)

**C1. Job Application — Café**
You saw an advertisement for a part-time café position. Write a letter to the café manager:
- Introduce yourself.
- Explain why you are interested in the job.
- Describe your relevant experience.

**C2. Wrong Item Delivered**
You ordered a laptop online but received the wrong model. Write a letter to the online store:
- Describe the item you ordered.
- Explain what went wrong.
- Say what you want them to do (refund / exchange).

**C3. Lost Luggage**
An airline lost your luggage during a recent flight. Write a letter to the airline:
- Give the flight details.
- Describe the luggage and its contents.
- State what compensation you expect.

**C4. Incorrect Bill**
You received an incorrect electricity bill for your apartment. Write a letter to the utility company:
- Describe the bill and the error.
- Explain why the bill is wrong.
- Ask them to correct it and reply.

**C5. Course Information**
You want to enroll in an English course at a language center. Write a letter to the center:
- Introduce yourself and your goal.
- Ask about course times and fees.
- Ask what you need to do to register.

**C6. Restaurant Improvement (praise + suggestion)**
You recently had a good meal at a local restaurant, but the service was slow. Write a letter to the manager:
- Describe your positive experience.
- Point out the service problem.
- Suggest how the service could improve.

**C7. Public Transport Complaint (to the city council)**
Buses in your area are often late or crowded. Write a letter to the local transport authority:
- Explain the problem in your area.
- Describe how it affects residents.
- Suggest what should be done.

**C8. Gym Membership Refund**
You paid for a gym membership but cannot use it because the gym closed temporarily. Write a letter to the gym:
- Explain your situation.
- Ask about your membership options.
- Request either a refund or an extension.

## D. Tech / Business Letters (career-connection stretch — Band 5.5+)

These tie into `reaserching-diary` topics (AI, software, business) — use them when comfortable.

**D1. AI Training Proposal**
You believe your department needs training on AI tools to work faster. Write a letter to your manager:
- Describe the problem in your current workflow.
- Propose a short training session on a specific AI tool.
- Suggest how this improves team efficiency.

**D2. Conference Request**
You want to attend a technical conference for developers. Write a letter to HR:
- Describe the conference and its dates.
- Explain how it will benefit the company.
- Estimate the cost and ask for approval.

**D3. Client Delay Notice**
Your team is behind schedule on a client project. Write a letter to the client:
- Apologize for the delay.
- Explain the reason clearly.
- Give a new deadline and a plan to avoid further delay.

**D4. Software Bug / License Issue**
Your company's software license expires soon and a renewal invoice is missing. Write a letter to the vendor:
- Introduce your company and account.
- Explain the missing invoice and risk of service interruption.
- Request a copy of the invoice and extended grace period.

**D5. Remote Work Proposal**
You want a flexible remote-work arrangement for part of the week. Write a letter to your supervisor:
- Explain why you propose it (commute time, focus).
- Describe how your work will not be affected.
- Suggest a trial period to evaluate it.
