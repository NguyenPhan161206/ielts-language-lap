## Part 1 — Classify the following topics

**Instructions:** Classify each topic by **1) recipient / tone** and **2) purpose of the letter**.

| # | Topic | Recipient → Tone | Purpose |
|---|-------|------------------|---------|
| 1 | Moving to a new city for work → letter to a friend (plans + advice + arrange meeting) | **Informal** (friend) | Providing Information / Request |
| 2 | Unhappy with hotel service → letter to hotel manager | **Formal** (manager) | Complaint + Request for action |
| 3 | Lost a borrowed book → letter to teacher | **Semi-formal** (teacher) | Apology |
| 4 | Enrolled in part-time course → request schedule change to employer | **Formal** (employer) | Request |
| 5 | Job advertisement for café position → letter to café manager | **Formal** (manager) | Job Application |
| 6 | Surprise birthday party for sibling → invite cousin | **Informal** (relative) | Invitation |
| 7 | Ordered laptop, received wrong model → letter to online store | **Formal** (store) | Complaint + Request |

**🔑 Key logic:**
- **Recipient decides the tone:** friend/relative → informal (contractions, casual vocab); manager/store/employer → formal (no contractions, polite modal verbs `would / could / please`); teacher → semi-formal.
- **Purpose decides the structure:** every letter opens by stating its purpose, then gives details in the order of the 3 bullets, then closes politely.

**🔑 Suggested answer key (with logic):**

| Gap | Answer | Why |
|-----|--------|-----|
| (1) | **G** | Opening — states the purpose of the whole letter. |
| (2) | **H** | Leads into the first detail block (itinerary / training venue). |
| (3) | **D** | Introduces the accommodation paragraph. |
| (4) | **C** | Continues accommodation details (breakfast = hotel info). |
| (5) | **B** | Bridges into the airport pickup paragraph. |
| (6) | **F** | Follows pickup info; reminds about check-in requirement. |
| (7) | **E** | Closing — polite offer to help further. |
| (8) | **A** | Final sign-off nicety — ends the letter warmly. |

**📌 Pattern to remember (letter skeleton):**
1. **Opening:** G — purpose + polite intent.
2. **Body:** C, D, B, F — each detail paragraph is *introduced* by a topic sentence.
3. **Closing:** E, A — help offer + polite ending.

## Part 3 — Analyze the type of each topic, then make an outline

### Topic 1
> Write a letter to the HR department to provide information about a training session you attended.
> In your letter:
> - Describe the training session.
> - Explain how it helped you.
> - Suggest how it could benefit others.

**Type:** Formal (recipient = HR department); purpose = **Providing Information** (a report-type letter).

**Outline (Task 1 — 4-part skeleton):**
```
1. Greeting:       Dear Ms./Mr. [Name],  (or: Dear Human Resources Department,)
2. Opening:        I am writing to provide information about the [name] training session
                   I attended on [date] in [place].
3. Body Paragraph 1 (Describe training):  duration + topics covered + trainers/format
4. Body Paragraph 2 (How it helped me):  new skills/knowledge + example of applying it
                   to my daily work
5. Body Paragraph 3 (Benefit others):    recommend same training for colleagues + why
6. Closing:        I would be happy to share my notes / answer any questions.
7. Sign-off:       Yours faithfully,  [Full Name]
```
🎯 **TA check:** 3 bullets → 3 body paragraphs, each introduced with one topic sentence.

### Topic 2
> Write a letter to your supervisor to provide information about a recent client meeting.
> In your letter:
> - State when and where the meeting happened.
> - Mention the purpose of the meeting.
> - Summarize the discussion and results.

**Type:** Formal (recipient = supervisor); purpose = **Providing Information** (report).

**Outline:**
```
1. Greeting:       Dear [Supervisor's name],
2. Opening:        I am writing to report on the client meeting that took place on
                   [date] at [place].
3. Body Paragraph 1 (When & where):
                   date + time + location + who attended
4. Body Paragraph 2 (Purpose):
                   main goal of the meeting (e.g., signature, negotiation, review)
5. Body Paragraph 3 (Discussion & results):
                   key points of discussion + decisions/outcomes + next steps
6. Closing:        Please let me know if you would like more details.
7. Sign-off:       Best regards / Yours sincerely,  [Name]
```

## Part 4 — Write an essay/letter for each topic based on the outlines

### Model Letter 1 (Topic 1) — ≈150 words, Band 5.5–6.0

> Dear Human Resources Department,
>
> I am writing to provide information about the project management training session I attended last week at the Singapore office.
>
> The training lasted three days and covered scheduling, budgeting, and team communication. It was led by an experienced consultant, and all materials were provided during the course.
>
> The session helped me a lot. I have already started using the scheduling templates in my current project, which has made planning much clearer. I also learned how to communicate tasks more effectively with my team.
>
> I believe this training could benefit other staff members. Since many of my colleagues are involved in projects, they would gain the same practical skills. I would recommend offering the same session to the whole department.
>
> I would be happy to share my notes or answer any questions about the training.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Nguyen Phan Huu Binh

### Model Letter 2 (Topic 2) — ≈150 words, Band 5.5–6.0

> Dear Mr. Tuan,
>
> I am writing to report on the meeting I had with our client, TechStart, on Friday 18th September at their office in District 3, Ho Chi Minh City.
>
> The purpose of the meeting was to review the project progress and discuss the next phase of the software development contract.
>
> During the discussion, we confirmed that the first module is on schedule. The client was satisfied with the current results, but they asked for two small changes to the reporting interface. We agreed to deliver these changes by the end of October. Both sides also agreed to hold a follow-up meeting in November.
>
> As a result, we should update the project plan slightly and inform the development team about the new requirements.
>
> Please let me know if you would like to discuss any details in person.
>
> Best regards,
> Nguyen Phan Huu Binh
